The plan in one paragraph

TikTok brings new fans in, X is where they hear it from us first, and YouTube is where they learn to do it and find us through search. Everything is Arabic-first, made around real clubs, real grounds and real match weeks, and sounds like a fan talking. Every post has one job: get a fan to open the site and see their own club's stand. The next six weeks build up to the Asian Cup opener in Riyadh on 7 January.

Each platform's job

X

The official word

Announcements, new grounds, features, events and Asian Cup news. Match-night reactions within three hours of a real tifo. Tifo of the Week every Friday. Fifteen minutes a day replying to fans and fan groups.

TikTok

New fans

Short, loud, Arabic. Club rivalry does the work: "comment your club", derby duels, build timelapses, card counts. Comments become the next videos.

YouTube

Found by search

Every TikTok again as a Short, plus a long video every two weeks: tutorials and ground flyovers that people find by searching "تيفو" and ground names.

What we post about

  1. Your club. One club at a time, in its own ground, named in the post. Rotate fairly through the league.
  2. Reveal nights. When a fan group raises a real tifo, we credit it and add the one thing only we know: roughly how many cards it took.
  3. How it's made. Seat-by-seat builds, card counts, block names, ground measuring. The careful work is the content.
  4. Fans' tifos. The best community designs, credited, every week.
  5. Big moments. Derbies and the Asian Cup, built around Al-Akhdar so every club's fans join in.
  6. News. New grounds and features, on X, written as what a fan can now do.

How we learn what works

  1. Every Monday, paste me the week's analytics screenshots from the three apps.
  2. I log each post: views, viewers still there at 1 and 3 seconds, average watch, shares, saves, replies and site visits.
  3. Anything above our own average gets a reason written next to it (the hook? the club? the time?) and a follow-up in the same shape the next week.
  4. Anything below gets one change only, so we know what fixed it.

Use a separate link on each profile so the site can tell where visitors came from: tifomaker.org/?utm_source=tiktok, ?utm_source=x and ?utm_source=youtube.

Targets for the first six weeks

  • TikTok: a video keeps 60% of viewers past 3 seconds (video 1 lost more than half in the first second).
  • TikTok: 15 or more comments per 1,000 views on the "comment your club" series.
  • X: every question post gets 25 or more real replies.
  • Site: visits with a social source each week, rising week on week.
  • Asian Cup: 100 designs made on the King Fahd ground by 7 January.

What took off, and why

Al-Hilal's "3PTS" tifo, King's Cup final, May 2026

A Steph Curry three-pointer with "3PTS" written across it. It was picked up by Marca, Globo and The Athletic, which almost never cover Saudi stands.

Why it worked: a pun with a point (three points), and a star from outside football, which gave foreign media a reason to write about it.

What we take from it: when a tifo means something, say the meaning in one line. Our reveal posts (x-07) do that.

Al-Hilal's "hidden tifo" against Al-Ittihad, October 2026

The group announced only the stand and the colours, white, blue and black. News sites wrote whole articles about it before it went up.

Why it worked: anticipation and a guessing game. Fans argued about it for days.

What we take from it: before big games, ask fans what they would put on the stand (x-03), then draw the best answer.

Our own first TikTok

246 views, 88% from the For You feed. Average watch 4.1 of 14.4 seconds, only 4.6% finished, and the biggest drop at 0:01. 70% Saudi audience, but the video was in English.

Why it stalled: TikTok did show it to people. The first second didn't hold them: slow start, wrong language, read like an ad.

What we take from it: the best moment comes first, Arabic on screen, club names in the first three seconds.

How the platforms rank posts in 2026

  • TikTok: how much of the video people watch, then shares and saves, then likes. More people now find videos through TikTok search, so captions should use the words fans type ("تيفو الهلال").
  • X: replies count most, and X scores how good a reply is, so one-word replies count for little. Links in the main post are widely reported to reduce reach. We'll test that ourselves.
  • YouTube Shorts: each Short is first shown to a small group. If they swipe away, it stops there. Channels that stick to one subject get recommended more easily.

Most of these claims come from marketing blogs, not the platforms themselves. We test them against our own numbers.

How we sound

Like a fan talking in the stands or the group chat after a match, not a company. If a line could be on any brand's account, rewrite it.

  • Short lines. One idea each.
  • Name the club, the ground, the block. "The north stand at Al-Awwal Park" beats "your stadium".
  • Gulf Arabic, the way people text. Not formal news Arabic.
  • Western digits in both languages: 60,000, 3D, C3.
  • One emoji where a fan would use one: 🔥 👏 👇 and club hearts. Never a row of them.
  • Banter is fine. Insulting a club, its fans or its players is not.
  • Always credit the fan group or designer by their handle.
  • Never pass off a render as a real photo. Say it's drawn in TifoMaker if it could be mistaken.
