# Research brief: what makes world-class tifos great

Companion to `docs/AI-QUALITY-REVIEW.md`. Gathered 28 October 2026 by a
research pass over roughly 45 web-search results in English, German, Polish,
Italian, Arabic, Persian, Croatian/Serbian and French.

**Method caveat.** Full-page fetches were refused by the session's network
policy for every news and fan site tried (planetfootball, givemesport, ESPN,
ultras-tifo.net, cardstunts.com, Wikipedia, spl.com.sa and others), so every
claim below rests on search-result snippets, not full articles or photographs.
Claims resting on one social post are flagged. A second pass with those hosts
allowed would let us pull the photographs themselves, which is what the
exemplar library in the plan needs.

## 2. Recurring compositional patterns

**Colour count.** Card displays run on 2-4 colours: Celtic used green base plus
silver plus gold; Al Hilal's 2026 hidden tifo is "three main colours: white,
blue, black"; Kivett notes a card stunt with more than two colours needs more
than one card per seat; Bravara's example "GO TEAM" needs three colours
([Kivett process](https://www.cardstunts.com/process/),
[Bravara](https://blog.bravara.com/stadium-card-stunts-fans-unite-for-epic-displays/)).
Painted banners carry more tones but remain posterised flat shapes.

**Three layouts dominate.**

1. *One giant painted image filling the end stand* (Dortmund binoculars, Legia
   soldier, Copenhagen cannon, Lech child, Pennywise). The image fills the full
   stand height; any text is one line, usually along the bottom or on a separate
   strip.
2. *Card mosaic of text or a symbol* (Celtic, Legia countdown, Hilal). Cards
   carry only text, numbers, crests, stripes or geometric blocks.
3. *Whole-bowl split by role*: hero image on the curva/end, short slogan on the
   long main stand, flat colour or stripes on the remaining stands (Olympiacos:
   figure north, text main, stripes elsewhere; Celtic: text on North and Main,
   symbols on the two ends; Marseille four-stand anniversaries; Red Star 1996).

**Placement rules of thumb.** Hero in the middle of the end stand, centred; text
on the long side (where the TV cameras face) or on the front rail/balcony between
tiers; sides carry stripes or a flat club colour. Italian curve routinely run
"cartoncini tra Curva e Distinti" (cards across the curva and the adjacent
sector) with a cross or crest spanning both
([fcinternews](https://www.fcinternews.it/news/finale-di-coppa-la-nord-prepara-la-coreografia-croce-di-milano-e-cartoncini-tra-curva-e-distinti-895209)).
Text-to-image ratio in the best examples is one short line (2-6 words) against
one image.

**Faces.** Faces are almost always painted banners (Pennywise, Ronaldo portraits,
the Legia soldier, Ahlawy's jailed members). Card mosaics handle text, numbers,
crests and large flat shapes. Painter guidance: "faces and hands are really hard
to make look good close up, but from 20 feet away it looks fine"; paint the light
base first, "dark outlines or sections last, as they can hide any imperfections"
([PTFC Moderates](https://ptfcmoderates.tumblr.com/post/140806984007/tifo-tips),
[107ist](https://107ist.org/game-day/tifo/how-to-make-two-sticks)). Posterised
3-5 tone portraits are the norm; 2 tones reads as a stencil.

**Overhead-banner fabrication constrains composition.** Polish sektorówka
guidance: the cloth's cut lines, rope tunnels and handles must not cross a face
or the key words of the slogan
([planning guide](https://www.damskie-torebki.com.pl/oprawa-meczowa-poradnik)).

**Choreography = sequence.** Cards up on the launcher's signal and held ("alza
il cartoncino al suo segnale, resta fermo e abbassalo solo quando ti verrà
comunicato"; Dortmund: hold the cardboard until kick-off), then the overhead
banner unfurls, then pyro/smoke
([Calcio Napoli 24](https://www.calcionapoli24.it/notizie/napoli-milan-la-curva-a-preannuncia-la-coreografia-diffuse-le-istruzioni-per-stasera-n683693.html)).
Multi-stage examples: Legia's 5-4-3-2-1 countdown; Raja's five-in-one ending on
the infinity sign; Red Star's curtain opening; Hajduk's "first choreo with the
logo, followed by the banner". Kivett: a card stunt should last 60 s to 3 min.

**Physical units.** Cards 70 × 50 cm, fireproof (Cagliari supplier); 27,500
double-sided panels for the Südtribüne; cards are pre-sorted into labelled
sector packs by row and seat
([nontex](https://www.nontex.eu/anche-a-san-siro-arriva-il-tnt-di-nontex/),
[Monster Mesh](https://monster-mesh.co.uk/news/football-tifo-guide/)).

## 3. Principles and vocabulary from ultras and designers

**Vocabulary.**

- English/MLS: tifo, card display / card stunt, mosaic, overhead (pass-over)
  banner, two-stick / 2-pole, pole flags, section leader, cue card, pyro.
- German (DFB glossary): Choreo, Pappen/Papptafeln (cards), Blockfahne (block
  flag covering a block, passed over heads), Schwenkfahne (swung flag, stock over
  1.5 m), Doppelhalter (two-stick), Zaunfahne (fence banner), Bengalo
  ([DFB glossary PDF](https://www.dfb.de/fileadmin/_dfbdam/77794-Glossar_Fan-Utensilien.pdf)).
- Polish: oprawa (the whole display), kartoniada (card display), sektorówka
  (sector banner), transparent, flagi na kijach, gniazdowy (capo)
  ([RMF24](https://www.rmf24.pl/sport/news-ultrasi-gniazdowy-i-sektorowka-czyli-mala-sciagawka-z-kibico,nId,1681917)).
- Italian: coreografia, cartoncini, telone/telo, striscione, bandierone,
  lanciatori (callers), torce.
- Arabic: تيفو (tifo), دخلة (dakhla, the "entrance" display), ألتراس,
  رابطة/مجلس الجماهير (fans' league/council), كابو.
- Serbian/Croatian: koreografija, šetalica (the walking/moving banner), bakljada
  (flare show).

**Rules of thumb cited by practitioners.**

- Bold, high-contrast shapes; "avoiding fine details that may not render clearly
  at scale" ([Bravara](https://blog.bravara.com/stadium-card-stunts-fans-unite-for-epic-displays/)).
  A DFB fan-club card choreo "was too fine-grained, so it was hard to make out
  anything, and not every spectator took part", which led to simpler designs
  afterwards ([turus.net](https://www.turus.net/sport/fussball/ich-bin-voll-ey-der-dfb-fan-club-und-seine-teils-verrueckten-choreos)).
- Planners model "the curve of the bleachers, sightlines from the press box, even
  how the sun hits the crowd"; Kivett superimposes the design on a photo of the
  venue as the acceptance check ([Kivett](https://www.cardstunts.com/process/)).
- Legibility: the sign-industry baseline is 1 inch of cap height per 10 ft of
  viewing distance (25 mm per 3 m)
  ([banners.com](https://banners.com/c/how-far-away-can-someone-read-a-banner-complete-banner-font-size-guide)).
  Across a bowl (120-180 m) that is 1.2-1.8 m minimum for high-contrast print; a
  seat-pixel mosaic with imperfect uptake needs a multiple of that, in practice
  8-15 rows of letter height, which matches what the canon shows and what this
  engine measured (8 rows Latin, 12 Arabic).
- Process: themes months out; committee approval; secrecy until unveiling;
  paint-by-numbers grid painting (Charlotte: six hours with ten people); fans
  "don't know where they sit in a choreo" so instructions go on every seat
  ([Monster Mesh](https://monster-mesh.co.uk/news/football-tifo-guide/),
  [Continental story](https://www.continental-reifen.de/about-us/stories/der-herr-der-choreografien/)).
- Message: short, in-group, provocative or commemorative; the best are a single
  idea (binoculars, cannon, pig, countdown).

## 4. Saudi Pro League / MENA specifics

- **History and organisation.** Al Hilal fans are credited (2015 press) as the
  first in Saudi Arabia and Asia to run full-stadium tifos; Al Ittihad's
  match-day director claims the club "was actually the first" and has done tifos
  for ~15 years. Named groups: Blue Power (Al Hilal), Curva Gold (Al Ittihad), the
  Al Nassr fans' council (مجلس جماهير النصر). An ultras-watch account notes Saudi
  tifos "are often commissioned by clubs and paid for and produced by companies
  or external supporter groups" (treat as commentary)
  ([slaati 2015](https://slaati.com/2015/06/13/p351165.html),
  [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/sports/inside-saudi-arabia-el-clasico-al-ittihad-al-hilal-football-is-life-fans-saudi-pro-league),
  [The National](https://thenationalnews.com/sport/football/2024/04/26/photo-essay-blue-power-al-hilals-vibrant-and-vocal-support)).
  The SPL itself curates "The Art of the Tifo" galleries each season.
- **Bilingual text is the house style.** English slogans ("Not Like Us",
  "MENTALITY", "Greatness is earned", "focus, fight and finish") sit next to
  Arabic ones (كن قوياً, هنا الهلال, عميد العالمية, كبير الرياض, ليلة الوفاء,
  نادي الوطن, أطلق العنان للوحش). Al Hilal's 2025-26 banner carried English with
  an Arabic translation underneath. Hero subjects are the star player (Ronaldo
  repeatedly), the club mascot (Ittihad's tiger), milestones (numbers 6, 900,
  200) and pop-culture references (Kendrick Lamar, Dexter).
- **Arabic text handling.** No source describes calligraphy workflows for card
  mosaics. What the examples show: Arabic words appear as 2-4 word phrases in
  heavy, geometric, kufi-like or bold naskh forms on painted banners; on card
  mosaics Arabic is rare and when used is a single short word. Implication for
  the product: card-mosaic Arabic must be rendered as a pre-shaped glyph bitmap
  (contextual forms, RTL, baseline joins preserved) with stroke width of at least
  2 seats, because broken joins make Arabic unreadable far faster than Latin.
- **Palettes.** Al Hilal blue/white (+black accent); Al Nassr yellow/blue; Al
  Ittihad yellow/black; Al Ahli green/white; Raja green/white/black; Wydad
  red/white; Al Ahly red/white; Zamalek white/red; Espérance red/yellow;
  Persepolis red.
- **Approval regimes.** Tunisia requires Interior Ministry approval of any tifo;
  the UAE Pro League vets every tifo; Al Ittihad was in dispute over a late
  "undertaking document" for a derby tifo design
  ([Al Bayan](https://www.albayan.ae/sports/football/analysis/36083),
  [nawaat](https://nawaat.org/2025/06/12/),
  [eremnews](https://www.eremnews.com/sports/gkibjfe)).
- **A Saudi lesson in robustness.** Al Ittihad's "ليلة الوفاء" tifo v Al
  Qadisiyah was a substitute: the original design was cancelled "because
  attendance in the third tier could not be guaranteed"
  ([Goal AR](https://www.goal.com/ar/)).

## 5. Famous failures and what went wrong

1. **Bayern v Liverpool, 13 Mar 2019** — an agency-made card display meant to
   read "Pack mas" (let's go) was read by the stadium and TV as "Fick mas".
   Letterforms that are distinct on paper collapse at seat resolution and from
   the camera angle; the fan scene disowned it
   ([watson](https://www.watson.de/sport/fussball/243639373-fc-bayern-muenchen-fc-liverpool-choreo-fail-warum-steht-hier-fick-mas)).
2. **HSV derby banner "verzweifeifelt"** — a misspelling painted into the banner
   ([watson](https://www.watson.de/sport/fussball/944783215-choreo-fail-beim-derby-hsv-fans-verzweifeifeln-an-der-rechtschreibung)).

prefer fan-authored, local, timely messages over generic polish; verify
orientation per section; test from the TV camera position, not just head-on.

## 6. Existing tools

- **Kivett Productions (cardstunts.com)** — the professional card-stunt vendor
  since the 1950s. Inputs: seating diagram, seating manifest, wide-angle photos.
  The venue is modelled in proprietary software, designs overlaid using exact
  PMS colours and superimposed on a venue photo for client sign-off.
  "Traditional" stunts = one solid colour per card; "Billboard" stunts = one image
  tile per card; more than two colours per seat means multiple cards. Output:
  instruction printed on each card, PA script, section leaders with cue cards,
  cards sorted by section/row, taped to seats. Duration 60 s to 3 min
  ([process](https://www.cardstunts.com/process/),
  [FAQ](https://www.cardstunts.com/process/faqs/)). Quality is judged by the
  photo-overlay preview, not by any computed metric.
- **Stadiugraph** — demo videos (c. 2015) of interactive stadium choreography
  planning; the site's design-and-perform workflow says "Coming Soon"
  ([stadiugraph.com](https://stadiugraph.com/)).
- **Phone-as-pixel apps**: TifoPix, People Pixel (Wichita State), Fannex, the
  "tifiz" hackathon project whose stated hard problem was "accurate, real-time
  fan positioning and perspective calibration".
- **PixMob** — IR-controlled LED wristbands, roughly 5-10 USD per head, 70,000
  units at the Paris 2024 closing ceremony
  ([PixMob MVT](https://pixmob.com/mvt)).
- **Consumer/novelty**: Tifos World, Tifo Forge (animated tifo from team +
  emotion + symbol), the 2006 "Tifo Generator" blog toy.
- Finding: **no tool found computes legibility, contrast, dropout robustness or
  TV-angle readability.** Kivett's photo overlay is the only quality gate in use.
  That is the gap this product's AI can own.

## 7. What an AI tifo designer should aim for (testable rules)

1. **One hero per bowl.** Exactly one image motif; it lives on an end stand and
   fills at least 80% of that stand's row height and 60% of its seat width,
   centred within ±2 seat columns of the stand's centre.
2. **Card layers use at most 4 colours per stand** (base + 3), and at most 6
   distinct card colours across the whole bowl; a deeper palette is for
   painted/generated-image layers only.
3. **Palette comes from club identity**: at most 3 club colours plus white and
   black for cards; any extra accent appears only inside the hero.
4. **Text is one line per stand, at most 5 words / 25 characters (Latin) or 4
   words (Arabic)**, placed on the long side stand or in a band at the front
   rail/tier balcony, never overlapping the hero.
5. **Letter cap height at least 8 rows; stroke width at least 2 seats; letter
   gap at least 1 seat; sans-serif, geometric glyphs only on cards.** Derived from
   the 25 mm per 3 m legibility rule with a 3× safety factor for seat-pixel
   rendering across 120-180 m.
6. **Arabic on cards is rendered from shaped glyph bitmaps** at stroke width of
   at least 2 seats; reject any word whose joins break at seat resolution.
7. **Spelling and glyph-ambiguity gate**: dictionary plus club lexicon check in
   both languages; then OCR the seat-resolution render from the TV-camera
   viewpoint and require the recovered string to match (the "Pack mas / Fick
   mas" test).
8. **Minimum feature size**: no shape thinner than 2 seats or smaller than 3 × 3
   seats survives; run a 1-seat morphological erosion and reject if any element
   disappears.
9. **Contrast**: adjacent colours in any layer pair differ by at least 50 L* (or
   4.5:1 luminance ratio); no two mid-tones touch.
10. **Outline rule**: any figure or letter whose fill is within 30 L* of its
    background gets a 1-2 seat outline in the darkest palette colour.
11. **Faces only in painted/generated layers.** If a portrait must be a card
    mosaic, posterise to at most 3 tones + background and require a canvas of at
    least 60 rows × 80 seats; otherwise fall back to a crest, number or
    silhouette.
12. **Gradients on cards are banded** into at most 3 steps; smooth gradients are
    reserved for painted layers.
13. **Tier boundaries are not crossed by letters or faces**; a text band sits
    wholly within one tier; walkways may carry a continuous painted strip but not
    card content.
14. **Whole-bowl coherence**: at most three motif types across the bowl (hero,
    text, stripes/flat), identical palette on every stand, side stands default to
    club-colour stripes or flat fill.
15. **Dropout robustness**: the design must stay recognisable with 15% random
    empty seats and 30% empty in the top tier (the Al Ittihad third-tier case);
    require the OCR/recognition test to pass on the degraded render.
16. **Perspective check**: render from the main-stand TV camera and from the
    opposite end; upper-tier content is pre-stretched vertically to counter
    foreshortening; both renders must pass rules 5-9.
17. **Thumbnail test**: downsample the stand render to 64 × 32 px; the hero must
    still be classifiable (crest/face/object) by a vision check.
18. **Choreography output**: every spec ships a 2-3 step sequence (cards up on
    signal, held at least 60 s through kick-off; overhead banner unfurl; optional
    pyro/smoke) and, where a multi-stage card display is used, each stage obeys
    rules 2-9 independently.
19. **Message quality**: local language first, English second (the Saudi
    bilingual pattern); a single idea; no dated memes; commemorations, milestones
    and rival taunts score higher than generic "come on" text.
20. **Fabrication sanity**: painted layers are single rectangles per stand within
    the stand's dimensions, with no cut line or rope tunnel passing through a face
    or the slogan; card counts per colour are reported per sector so the plan can
    be packed into sector bags.
