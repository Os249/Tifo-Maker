Tifo Maker · where the AI lives

Four ways to give AI Create and AI Critique one home. Every mock-up uses the editor's own tokens and fonts and is drawn in Arabic, right to left, at desktop and phone width. Panels scroll. Nothing here is production code.

Two names, two verbs«صمّم» (Create) makes a new tifo and replaces what is on the stands. «راجِع» (Critique) keeps your tifo and suggests fixes. Each has its own icon and its own counter in every option.
Apply is a tint, never blueBlue stays for the one next step (Save, then Post it). «طبّق» is a tinted button, «تجاهل» a text button, and an applied card turns into «تم التطبيق · تراجع».
Counters before you spendThe button that starts a review says what it costs («تستخدم 1 من 2»). Create, Critique and chat messages each have a meter and a reset time.
Numbers are placeholders3 reviews and 20 messages a week (regular), 10 and 60 a day (Pro) stand in until the real token cost is measured. Create's 10 a day and 15 a day are today's real limits.

A · A tab in the side panel the smallest change

The properties panel gets an AI tab. A two-way switch at the top flips between «صمّم» and «راجِع», each with a four-word subtitle. Counters sit under the switch, fix cards stack below, and the chat box is pinned to the bottom of the panel. Pins on the tifo match the card numbers.

What works

  • Nothing new to learn: AI is where it is today, and the panel, its tabs and the phone sheet already exist.
  • The tifo is fully visible while cards are applied, so cause and effect are easy to see.
  • Cheapest to build and to test.

What doesn't

  • 300px is tight: before/after pictures are about 110px wide, and counters, intent, six cards, ideas and chat become one very long scroll with the chat far from the cards it talks about.
  • A switch says "two views of one thing". A fan who lands on the wrong side has no reason to notice; «صمّم» would replace their tifo.
  • The AI tab takes the place of the paint tools, so "apply a fix, then touch it up by hand" means going back and forth between tabs.
  • Three meters always open eat a fifth of the panel before any content.

B · An AI drawer with two doors chosen, with a full-screen button

One ✦ button on the tool rail opens a dedicated AI drawer, 380px wide, in the panel's place. Its home screen is two cards. Each says in one sentence what it does to your tifo, shows a small picture of the outcome, and carries its own counter before anything is spent. Picking one opens that tool with a back arrow; the drawer remembers the last one used. The AI bar on the canvas stays as a shortcut to Create. It always opens as the drawer; a button in its header grows it to the whole screen and back, and the editor remembers nothing about that between visits.

What works

  • The choice is made in words, at the moment it matters: "replaces what is on the stands" against "your tifo stays as it is". This is the only option that answers "which one do I need?" before a click can cost anything.
  • Each door carries its own counter and its own limit or sign-in state, so a spent Critique never looks like a spent Create, and the counter is seen before spending.
  • 380px gives before/after pictures that read (about 150px each) and keeps the chat box directly under the cards.
  • One home for future AI tools (camera check, cut-out) without another redesign.
  • Phone and desktop are the same structure: home, then a tool.

What doesn't

  • One more tap than a tab. Softened by remembering the last tool and by the AI bar going straight to Create.
  • At 1280px the drawer leaves about 840px for the stadium; on small laptops it should narrow to 340px.
  • The drawer covers the paint properties while open, as the AI tab does today.
  • More to build than A: a new drawer, a home screen and a rail button.

C · A dock under the stadium fixes right below the tifo

The AI opens as a dock along the bottom, in the place the seat strip takes in Split. Two stacked tabs and the counters sit on its edge, fix cards run sideways under the tifo, and the chat has its own column. The properties panel stays free for the paint tools.

What works

  • The fix sits right under the thing it fixes; eyes travel a few centimetres from card to stand.
  • The paint panel stays open, so applying a fix and touching it up by hand is one flow.
  • Chat is always visible beside the cards.

What doesn't

  • It fights the seat strip for the same place: in Split, opening the AI hides the strip, which is where most editing happens.
  • A sideways row hides most cards. Six fixes and two ideas mean scrolling horizontally, which mice do badly, and "6 fixes" stops being something you can scan.
  • It takes 45% of the stadium's height; the tifo being reviewed gets smaller.
  • The intent read is squeezed into one truncated line, the opposite of "shown to the user, who can correct it".
  • On a phone it collapses into a sheet anyway, so desktop and phone become two designs to keep.

D · A full-screen review mode Critique as its own place

Critique leaves the editor and takes the whole screen, the way Match Day does: the tifo large, with numbered pins and a before/after toggle, the fixes in a wide column, the chat under them. It opens from a «راجِع تيفوي» button beside Save. Create stays where it is today (the AI tab and the AI bar), so the two are told apart by where you find them.

Create's counter is not on this screen; it lives with Create.

What works

  • The most room: large previews, a whole-tifo before/after, and a wide column for cards and chat.
  • Create and Critique cannot be confused, because they are in different places.
  • A focused moment: nothing else on screen competes with the review.

What doesn't

  • It does not do what you asked for first: the AI is still in two places, and the two counters are never seen together.
  • Leaving the editor breaks the loop. To touch up an applied fix by hand you go back, edit, and come in again.
  • A second button beside Save competes with the one blue step, and a mode is heavier to enter for a quick question in chat.
  • Handing an idea to Create means jumping out of the mode and into the panel.
  • The most to build: a new mode with its own canvas, camera and phone page.

Recommendation: B, the AI drawer with two doors

The hardest requirement is that a fan never mixes up the tool that replaces their tifo with the tool that keeps it. A switch (A, C) asks them to read two small labels; separate places (D) solve it by scattering the AI again. B solves it in one place, in words, with a picture and a counter on each door, before anything is spent. It is also the only layout that is the same on a phone and a desktop.

Two things I would borrow into B:

  • From D, the numbered pins on the tifo and a "before / after all fixes" toggle on the canvas, so the stadium itself is the big preview.
  • From A, the option to dock the drawer at 340px on laptops under 1366px.

A floating chat bubble was considered and left out: it would sit over the stadium, which the design bible rules out for anything that is not a camera control, and a chat-first window blurs Create and Critique into one conversation.

One question for you whichever you pick: should chat messages have their own counter (as drawn), or should each review include a number of follow-up messages? The second is one less number for a fan to track; I'd decide once the real cost of a message is measured.
