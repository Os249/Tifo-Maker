Banners: place them on real blocks, and never bigger than the ground

Two things were wrong and they produced the same screenshot: a 48 x 28 m
sheet hanging off the top of a stand and into the skyline.

Placement was a slider. "Along" ran 0-100 across a stand, which gives a banner
no reason to line up with anything: it straddled aisles, sat half on and half
off a block, and near the corners it hung past the end of the ground. No crew
thinks that way. They think "the whole of block 4 and 5, upper tier", because
that is what a stand is divided into and what they can get people into.

Size was unbounded. The editor rightly lets you draw at any size — you are
designing artwork, not ordering fabric — but Match Day took the number
literally.

Blocks and tiers, found rather than invented
StandFrame now reports the stand's real structure. Blocks are the wedges of
seating between the radial aisles, found by looking for the gaps at 320 bins
across a stand rather than by dividing it into equal parts, so they land on
the aisles whatever a template does — a 1.2 m aisle on a 133 m stand is under
a percent of it, which is why the 56 columns the surface is built from could
not see them. The generic bowl gets 8 blocks per stand, the single kop 6, the
grand oval 8, the grand national 9.

Tiers are read off the seat map's tierOf. The first attempt looked for a
horizontal band with no seats in it and found one tier on every ground in the
catalogue including the two-tier ones: the walkway is a few metres out of
forty and the dense rows either side smear across it. The map already carried
the answer.

bannerFit.ts — one place decides where it goes and how big it may be
Pure: frame in, geometry out, no Three.js and no DOM. Nothing in the rig reads
doc.widthM, doc.heightM, doc.place.alongU or doc.place.heightV any more.
- A block run means exactly those blocks; height follows the design's own
  proportions.
- blockFrom -1 means centred, which is where a tifo goes and the only sane
  default when the block count depends on the ground.
- Scaled, never squashed: the artwork is normalised to the banner's width, so
  clamping the two dimensions independently would show a stretched version of
  the design the user drew.
- The reach is a different physical thing per type: terracing for a cover, the
  air in front of the stand for a roof-hung one, 9 m of pole for a pole banner,
  the height of the fence for a fence banner, the grass for a pitch banner.
- The panel says "48x28 m does not fit here — cut to 18x11 m". Silently
  resizing someone's artwork is worse than not fitting it.
Old scenes keep the free placement they were saved with.

In the panel: Stand, then Blocks and Tier, with the free sliders kept as a
fallback that hides itself. Dragging a block-placed banner moves it a block at
a time. The editor asks the built bowl for the block list through a
tifo:stand-slots event, since only the simulator knows.

Three bugs this turned up
- A banner tied to a curved rail follows the rail. The anchored edge was laid
  out as a straight chord from a single tangent — fine mid-stand, wrong at the
  corners, where chord and rail part company by metres. Measured on a rope
  lift: 0.04 mm per frame on the middle blocks, 2.5 mm on the corner ones.
- But a banner flown from the roof does NOT: it hangs from a straight cable
  and has no reason to inherit the stand's shape.
- A flown banner's perimeter is pinned, not lashed. Its net is rigged taut
  between roof points and we do not simulate the net's own movement; lashing
  left every anchor drifting inside a 2 cm ball and 1400 particles drifting
  with them, 5 mm per frame in dead still air.

Also: a failure to mount the Banner view was swallowed by a bare
.catch(() => null), leaving a blank rectangle and no clue why. It now says so.

Gated: three grounds x four stands x eight kinds, asking for 400 x 120 m and
checking the result is inside the slot, reported as cut, keeps its proportions
to 2%, and that blocks do not overlap, cover no more than the stand, and a
named run lands on the blocks it names.
