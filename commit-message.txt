Banners: lace the perimeter, make the net structural, stop the shiver

The banner was out of the stand and correctly timed, and it vibrated. No
screenshot shows that and no census sees it, so the first thing built was a
measuring stick: npm run probe:banner, reporting three numbers per type.

  flat   RMS distance from the fabric's own best-fit plane
  buzz   mean per-FRAME vertex movement — this is jitter
  drift  net movement over a second — this is sway, and a banner should
         have some

Buzz and drift had been one number, and that number could not tell a banner
swaying on its ropes from a banner vibrating. At the top of the wind slider a
fence banner was moving 1.5 m PER FRAME and a centre-circle banner 0.67 m.

What the research changed
A tifo banner is held at many points and is never a sheet hanging from one
edge. Eyelets go in every 50 cm; the hem has a rope sewn into it; a
supporters' group describing their own build says they "grommet around the
edges and attach to the field goal net with zip ties"; the DFB's glossary
defines a fence flag as one FIXED to the railings. Engineering analyses of
banners under wind agree from the other end: the load is "dominated by the
tension or catenary forces", which is only true of something anchored all
round.

- Lashings: a particle tied to a point with a couple of centimetres of play,
  which is literally a grommet on a zip tie. Every rigged type is now laced
  along its whole perimeter; the two a crowd carries are held across their
  whole area instead. The slack is geometrically amplified — 9 cm a side on a
  20 m banner buys a metre of belly, 2 cm buys 40 cm, a roped hem buys none.
- The net became structural. It was being DRAWN and not enforced: ropes lying
  obediently over the surface of a bulge they should have prevented. Four
  rope clamps per particle to the perimeter of its row and column. What that
  buys is reach, not stiffness — local constraints move information one cell
  per sweep, so on a 27-row sheet under seventy times its own weight the
  tension never arrives. Belly on a 36x18 m rope lift: 2.9 m -> a few cm.
- The bowl is a wind shadow. Wind falls to a fifth at pitch level, and fabric
  resting on something sits in the boundary layer at a quarter of free stream,
  a tenth under a crowd.

Four more bugs, each found by a number
- The rope lift had 1.7 m of slack built in: its rail anchor was cleared of
  the crowd and its batten, 18 m up in open air, was not, so two pins 16.3 m
  apart held 18 m of cloth.
- More substeps made the shimmer WORSE, 0.030 -> 0.075 m/frame in still air.
  Backwards for a convergence problem, and the signature of an injection once
  per substep: the net clamps and the tethers moved p without q. Every
  unilateral constraint is now inelastic.
- A constraint sitting exactly on its limit chatters. A flat sheet puts the
  net ropes precisely at length. A tenth of a percent of rope stretch, honest
  for a lightly loaded polyester net, puts the rest state inside the limit.
- A bend dead zone was tried and reverted: it let the sheet crease freely
  inside the margin and shimmer went up two orders of magnitude.

Damping 0.18 -> 0.9 per second: not a fudge for instability, which was the
sweep order, but the structural damping a seamed, laced, net-backed sheet has.

Rigging corrected
A pole banner is made off at its bottom edge at the rail like a rope lift. A
roof-hung banner flies in free air in front of the terracing — hung above the
middle of a rake it is inside the seating within two metres, and what came out
of that was a banner draped over the seats being called roof-hung.

Result, at full wind
  fence     1.519 -> 0.000 m/frame      pitch   0.673 -> 0.000
  drop      0.113 -> 0.00014            lift    0.054 -> 0.00033
  pole-out  0.008 -> 0.00004            roof    0.002 -> 0.00027
and flatness: drop 0.76 -> 0.05, lift 1.06 -> 0.26, fence 0.34 -> 0.02,
pitch 0.45 -> 0.001, pole-out 0.31 -> 0.001.

26 shots: 0.000 m inside the stand and 0.000 m of sag on every one. A rendered
clip was watched frame by frame rather than trusted as stills.
