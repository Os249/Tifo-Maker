Banners: replace the parametric sheet with a real cloth solver

The first version held a banner off the stand with a fixed normal offset.
Against a raked, stepped surface that is not a collision, it is a hope, and
it failed exactly the way it had to: the sheet cut straight through the
terracing. The reveals were worse — every one of them ran for 4.2 seconds,
which is about right for a drop and roughly ten times too fast for a rope
lift.

The shot harness did not catch either, and that is the part worth recording.
It checked that the RIG existed — ropes, net, bar, poles — and framed every
screenshot from 31 degrees up, which is the one elevation where a banner
buried in the seating looks identical to one resting on it.

Physics
- standHeightfield.ts bakes a stand into a heightfield half-space sampled
  from the same pointAt() the geometry uses, so the collider and what the
  user sees cannot drift apart. Two array reads per test, cheap enough to
  run on every particle of every substep.
- cloth.ts is substepped XPBD: zero-compliance distance constraints,
  unilateral bend, LRA tethers, Coulomb friction, and aerodynamic load from
  areal density. Mass, drag and gust period are all real numbers.
- bannerRig.ts drives the solver instead of a surface function. The layout
  WALKS the sheet down row by row and projects out of the collider at each
  step, so it hangs where there is air and drapes where there is terracing.

Three bugs the measurement found that no screenshot would have
- The Gauss-Seidel sweep direction alternated every substep. One iteration
  does not converge, velocity is (p-q)/h, so the residual flipped sign at
  the substep frequency and the velocity feedback pumped it: 0.2, 1.6, 9.2,
  52, 446 m/s over five frames on a 3x3 sheet with nothing but gravity.
  Fixed order, and the same test sits still to five decimals.
- Collision moved p but not q, so the push-out was handed back as velocity;
  and with no velocity-level pass, a particle resting on a surface kept the
  velocity driving it in. A corner reached 24 m/s while not moving at all.
- Aero load used the grid cell area for every particle. A corner owns a
  quarter of a cell and a quarter of the mass, so its edges took off at four
  times the acceleration of the fabric they were attached to.

Reveals from deployment speeds, not a curve
physicalRevealMs() derives each one: a drop is free fall plus settle (5.0 s),
a rope lift is its height hauled at 0.5 m/s (40 s), a crowd pass is the block
at 1.0 m/s (28 s), a roof hoist is 0.35 m/s (43.7 s). verify holds each to
the band real deployments fall in. The seconds slider is now a power curve
spanning 0.4 s to two minutes and prints the real figure beside the chosen
one.

Rigging corrected
A rope lift and a pole banner are made off at their BOTTOM edge at the front
rail and built upward, which is what an Aufziehfahne is. A pole banner cannot
be propped out from the face of a stand at all — over the 8 m it drops, a 30
degree rake falls away 14 m — so it stands at the rail and leans out over the
moat, and it carries neither net nor ropes, because two poles are the rig.

A banner over a full kop rests on people
Fifteen hundred of them, about 1.7 m above the treads, and they grip. Both
scale with crowd fill and are read live, so emptying the stand lowers the
sheet onto the seats while the user watches.

Measurement
The harness now reports metres inside the stand and metres of sag, settles by
stepping the solver rather than sleeping, shoots every type from 6 degrees as
well, and checks the banner's bounding box so a crumple cannot pass as a
banner. All 26 shots: 0.000 m inside the stand. verify gained a headless
physics gate that needs no browser.
