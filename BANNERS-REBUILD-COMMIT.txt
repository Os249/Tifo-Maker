=== SUMMARY ===

Banners: rebuild on a finite, provable model


=== DESCRIPTION ===

(This covers the WHOLE branch. Your repo is at c68eef3, so the commit you make
contains all four of my commits at once, not just the last one.)

The last four rounds all failed the same way: a banner was fine where I tested it and broken where you went. That is not a bug pattern, it is what an infinite configuration space looks like from the inside. A free position and a size in metres made the set of states a banner could be in continuous, so it could be sampled and never proved.

So the model changed rather than the code. A banner now has a SLOT — a stand, a run of the blocks that stand is divided into, and a tier — and no size at all: it is as wide as the blocks it covers, and as deep as its artwork's proportions make it. That set is finite, so `npm run sweep:banner` enumerates ALL of it — 86,528 configurations across the catalogue — and checks eleven properties of each. It runs in a few minutes without a browser.

The shape is a pure function P(slot, u, v, t). For the sheet on the terracing every vertex is a point of the stand's own surface pushed out along that point's normal by a strictly positive amount, so it can no more be inside the seating than a decal can be inside a wall — for any size, any block, any tier, any ground, any frame. Nothing accumulates, so nothing resonates or explodes, and the same inputs give the same vertices to the bit. That deletes the cloth solver, the baked heightfield, the rope net and the lashing pass: 4,512 lines out, 2,908 in.

Two types, both perfected: a stand banner over a run of blocks, and a hanging banner flown on ropes. A banner can stretch across two stands, carrying on round the corner into the next one.

MEASUREMENT BUGS THE SWEEP FOUND BEFORE ANYTHING WAS RENDERED

- Arc length along a stand DIVERGED with sampling. The same 48 m rake measured 48 m at 8 steps, 75 at 64 and 214 at 256, because the surface was threaded through every individual seat and picked up a seat's width of lateral jitter between consecutive points. Every "metres down the slope" figure depended on its step count. The "corner rake is four times the centre" fixed in the previous round was this artefact, not geometry. A column is now one point per ROW of seating, averaged, and it converges everywhere.

- Banner seams were spaced evenly in `u`, a fraction of the stand. On a ground whose end blocks are 3.7 m and middle blocks 29 m that put four columns across the tight corner and thirty across the straight, and the sheet cut 4.75 m inside the curve. They are spaced by metres of fabric now, which also stops the artwork being squashed at corners.

- A stand's ends double back on themselves in plan where a corner is cut hard, and the block detector was offering those four-metre curls as places to hang a banner. They are not blocks and are no longer offered.

- Two adjacent columns filled from the same neighbour gave a zero tangent, and a 33 m hanging banner rendered as a 1.5 m sliver.

- A height-limited banner reported 54 m and drew 103 m, because the size shrank and the mesh did not.

GEOMETRY BUGS THE RENDERED SHOTS FOUND AFTER

- A hanging banner anchored at the back row and hung down through every seat in the stand, so it did not appear in a single frame.

- A whole-stand banner pushed the camera 200 m out, behind the stand it was aimed at; in a roofed arena the default 31-degree elevation put it in the rafters. Both shots came back black.

- The stand's outward normal pointed DOWNWARD at eighty of eight hundred sample points on one ground — the cross product it was built from was flipped by a test on its horizontal component, which says nothing about its vertical one — so a banner offset along it sat three metres inside the seating. It is built by decomposing the rake tangent in the vertical plane now, which cannot point into the ground.

- The wave is two-sided, so at full wind it subtracted a metre from the standoff and put the sheet through the people holding it. It sits on a base raised by its own amplitude now, so the trough still clears. The clearance itself was wrong too: a person is a VERTICAL segment, and what that occupies along a tilted normal is its height times the normal's vertical component.

- A hanging banner floated 25 m out over the pitch: the geometry anchored it at the front rail while the standoff and the camera measured from the back row.

- The ropes ran straight up in world Y to a point in empty air, crossing the terracing, and were one-pixel lines. They are tied to the roof's leading edge now — or to the back rail where that ground has no roof — and are thin cylinders that catch the light.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE PANEL

Blocks, a tier, a shape and an across-stands choice, with the size as a readout in metres rather than a field to get wrong. "How many" stops where the banner stops growing, and says why. A tier is offered to a flown banner only when its fascia band is deep enough to hang anything in — measured across the catalogue that band is 3.3 to 3.5 m on most grounds, 0.8 m on two, and negative on one where the tiers overlap.

A hanging banner over the whole stand or the lower tier reaches the grass and is fixed there; over an upper tier it fills the gap between that tier and the one below. Its reveal is an Aufziehfahne — hauled UP its ropes from a fixed hem.

The retired Banner Studio's work is not migrated: its placement no longer exists, and inventing a slot for it would put it somewhere nobody chose.

THE GATES, AND WHAT EACH ONE'S ABSENCE COST

IN-STAND, EXTENT, CHORD, OFFSET, FINITE, SAME, STILL — plus three added after you found what they missed:

- MOVES — at full wind the fabric must travel at least 25 cm in a second. Every other check was an UPPER bound, so a banner that did not move at all passed them all.
- CROWD — every vertex clears the PEOPLE, not merely the concrete. A banner can be comfortably outside the terracing and still be through the chest of everyone holding it.
- HANG — a flown banner is inside the air it was given, and fixed at the end it is meant to be fixed at.

Partial reveals are swept too, at 37% as well as at rest: a sheet gathered near its top edge is a configuration the finished one never visits.
